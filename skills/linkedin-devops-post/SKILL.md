# LinkedIn post, engineer-grade

Input: what actually happened (PR, incident notes, metrics, a repo). Output: a post other engineers don't scroll past. If the input has no concrete detail, ask for one number or one failure first — the post stands on it.

## Structure that works

1. **Hook (1–2 lines):** the tension, not the moral. "Our CI bill doubled in a month. Nobody had shipped anything new." Specific beats clever; numbers beat both. The first 2 lines are all most people see — spend effort there.
2. **The middle (3–6 short paragraphs):** what you found → what you tried that *didn't* work → what worked → proof (number, graph, before/after). One idea per paragraph; line breaks are free, use them.
3. **The takeaway (1 line):** earned by the story above it, not imported from a quote site.
4. **Question to the room:** specific enough that an engineer can answer from experience. "How do you cap runner costs without queue times exploding?" — not "Thoughts?"

## Rules

- ~1,300 characters is the sweet spot; hard stop at 2,000. No "see more" cliff mid-sentence in the hook.
- 0–3 hashtags, at the end, niche over broad (#platformengineering over #technology).
- Numbers do the bragging: "cut p95 from 1.8s to 300ms" needs no adjectives.
- Failure sells better than success: the migration that went sideways teaches more than the one that didn't.
- Plain text; code snippets only if ≤3 lines. Link goes in the post if it's the point (your repo), first comment if it's a reference.

## Never write like this

Banned openers: "Thrilled to announce", "I'm humbled", "In today's fast-paced world", "Game-changer", "🚀 Excited to share". Banned patterns: emoji-bullet walls (✅✅✅), "Agree?", engagement-bait polls about obvious things, AI-tells ("delve", "it's not just X, it's Y", "Let that sink in").

Write like a senior engineer explaining something interesting to a peer over chai — because that's exactly what it is.

## Output

Deliver: the post, ready to paste · a one-line alternative hook · suggested posting note (best engagement: Tue–Thu, your morning). Never invent metrics or events that didn't happen — a fabricated number found out costs more than every post gained.
